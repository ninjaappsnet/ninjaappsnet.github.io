Diagrove is a diagramming app for the Mac and the iPad. This page takes you from opening it for the first time to a diagram on the canvas, and shows where everything in a window lives.

## What you need

- A Mac with macOS 26 or later, or an iPad with iPadOS 26 or later.
- Nothing else to start. There’s no account to make and nothing to set up.
- Apple Intelligence is optional. You need it only to describe a diagram in your own words. Steps such as `Idea -> Plan -> Ship`, and changes such as “make all decisions orange”, work without it. See [The prompt bar](../prompt-bar/).

## Start on the Mac: the Welcome window

When Diagrove opens with nothing to open, it shows the Welcome window. On the left are the ways to start:

- **New diagram** (Command-N): an empty canvas.
- **New isometric diagram** (Control-Command-N): an empty canvas in isometric projection. See [Isometric canvases](../isometric-canvases/).
- **Describe a diagram…** (Option-Shift-Command-N): a new document with the prompt bar ready for you to type.
- **Open…** (Command-O): a document you already have.

On the right, the first time, six templates wait under “Start with a template”: Flowchart, System architecture, App screen, Mind map, Roadmap and Org chart. Double-click one, or select it and press Return or click **Create**. Press Space for a large preview. **All templates…** (Shift-Command-N) opens the full gallery. See [Templates](../templates/).

Once you have documents, the right side has two tabs, **Recent** and **Templates** (Command-1 and Command-2). Choose a recent document and press Return to open it.

**Paper** chooses what new diagrams start on: White or Ink. A document keeps its paper in light and dark appearance alike, so you choose it once. On the Mac you can also change it in **Diagrove › Settings…**, under *New diagrams start on*.

To see the Welcome window again, choose **Window › Welcome to Diagrove** (Shift-Command-1). Turn off *Show this window when Diagrove opens* if you’d rather start with a new blank diagram.

## Start on the iPad

The start screen shows **New diagram** and **Choose a template** above the Files browser, where your documents are. Choose a template opens the gallery as a sheet: tap a template to start from it, or choose **Describe a diagram…** in the sheet’s bar.

A document you start from a template or with Describe a diagram… goes where you work: beside the last document you opened, or else in Diagrove’s folder in iCloud Drive when iCloud is on, or else in On My iPad › Diagrove. A document made from a template is named after it, such as “Flowchart”.

## The parts of a window

- **The tool palette** floats above the canvas: Select (V), Rectangle (R), Rounded rectangle, Ellipse (O), Diamond (D), More shapes, Line (L), Text (T) and Table. On an isometric canvas it offers iso shapes instead. See [Drawing](../drawing/).
- **The sidebar** has three tabs. **Canvases** lists the document’s canvases, with the current canvas’s layers below them. **Outline** shows the canvas as a tree of topics (see [Outline and auto-layout](../outline-and-auto-layout/)). **Stencils** holds the built-in stencils and your own libraries (see [Stencils and tints](../stencils-and-tints/)). On the Mac, **View › Hide Sidebar** is Control-Command-S. On iPad the sidebar sits beside the canvas in landscape and over it in portrait.
- **The inspector**, on the right, changes what’s selected: Fill, Stroke, Line, Text and Arrange, and for one graphic its Action and Data. With nothing selected it shows the canvas: its name, size, paper and projection, and the grid. Control-Command-I shows or hides it. In a narrow iPad window it opens as a sheet.
- **The prompt bar**, at the bottom of the canvas, draws a diagram from steps or a description, and changes the one in front of you. See [The prompt bar](../prompt-bar/).
- **The toolbar.** On the Mac: the sidebar toggle, Arrange, Export, Send to Dayfolio, Play, and the inspector toggle on its own at the end. **View › Customize Toolbar…** adds more, such as Zoom, Insert picture, Tidy, Group, Lock and New canvas. On iPad: Undo, Redo, Sketch, Insert picture, Arrange, Export, Play and Inspector.

## Saving and iCloud Drive

A Diagrove document is a `.diagrove` file. It holds its canvases, its pictures and a copy of every stencil it uses, so it opens the same on any Mac or iPad.

- **On the Mac,** choose **File › Save** (Command-S) to name a new document and choose where it goes, iCloud Drive included. Recent documents show in the Welcome window.
- **On iPad,** documents live in the Files app, on your iPad or in iCloud Drive. A document in iCloud Drive opens on your Mac too.

Documents stay where you save them. Diagrove sends nothing anywhere.

## Undo

Every change you make is one step you can undo: **Edit › Undo** (Command-Z) and **Edit › Redo** (Shift-Command-Z). The menu names the step, so you know what goes back. A change made through the prompt bar is one step too, once you apply it.

On iPad, Undo and Redo lead the toolbar, and Command-Z works with a hardware keyboard.
