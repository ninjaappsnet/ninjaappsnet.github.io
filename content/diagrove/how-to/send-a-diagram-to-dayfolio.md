Dayfolio is Diagrove’s sibling app from NinjaApps. Send to Dayfolio puts a diagram into a Dayfolio entry as two files: a picture of the canvas you’re on, and the whole Document, zipped.

## On iPad

1. Open the canvas you want to send.
2. Tap **Export** in the toolbar, then **Send to Dayfolio**.
3. In the share sheet, choose **Dayfolio**. In Dayfolio’s sheet, pick the journal (it asks when you have more than one) and save. Dayfolio takes both files into an entry there.

The share sheet holds a PNG of the canvas’s drawing at 2× (smaller for a very large drawing) and the Document as a `.diagrove.zip` file.

## On the Mac

Choose **File › Send to Dayfolio…**, or click **Send to Dayfolio** in the toolbar. The share menu offers the same two files. Dayfolio for Mac doesn’t take shared files yet, so it isn’t listed there. Send from your iPad instead: a Document saved in iCloud Drive opens there too.

## What Dayfolio shows today

In the entry you see the picture of the canvas, and the zipped Document as a file attached to the entry. The picture is a still image: it doesn’t change when you change the diagram, and its Actions don’t run. The zipped file is the whole Document; unzip it to open it in Diagrove again.

Showing a diagram inside an entry as a live, clickable embed needs Dayfolio’s side of the work, which isn’t released. More connections between the two come in later versions.

## Other ways between the two apps

- **Shortcuts.** Diagrove’s Create Diagram from Text and Export Canvas as Image actions work with Dayfolio’s own actions in the Shortcuts app.
- **Actions.** A graphic’s Run shortcut Action can run a shortcut that uses Dayfolio’s actions, such as New entry or Quick note. See [Run a shortcut from a diagram](../run-a-shortcut-from-a-diagram/).
- **Links.** A `diagrove://new?text=…` link, with the steps after `text=`, opens Diagrove with that diagram drawn, from any app that opens links.
