A stencil you place stays linked to its library, and one colour, its tint, recolours all of it: every face of an isometric server, or the body and accent of a flat icon, each in the right shade and lit from the top left.

## Before you place it

1. Open the **Stencils** tab in the sidebar.
2. In the **Tint** row above the tiles, choose one of the ten hues, or **Own colours** for the stencil’s own.
3. Drag a stencil onto the canvas. The tiles show the tint you chose, and so does what you drop.

On light paper a hue goes on as its light version, on dark paper as its dark version, and on an isometric canvas as the full hue.

[shot: tint-row] Mac, light. The Stencils tab with teal chosen in the Tint row and the Servers and racks tiles drawn in teal. Point to the Tint row.

## After you place it

1. Select the stencil, or several of them.
2. In the inspector’s **Stencil** section, choose a colour in **Tint**. **None** gives it back its own colours.
3. For people, the same section has **Skin**, and **Hair** where the stencil has hair.

An isometric shape you drew with the tool palette, such as a block, takes its tint in the **Isometric** section instead.

[shot: stencil-section] Mac, dark appearance. An isometric person selected, with the inspector’s Stencil section showing Tint, Skin, Hair and Turn. Point to Tint.

## Change one part

1. Double-click a part of the stencil away from its words. You’re now working inside it, with that part selected.
2. Change the part’s fill or stroke in the inspector.

The Stencil section then counts what you changed, such as “2 parts changed · 1 hidden”. **Reset** gives every part the stencil’s look again and keeps the tint; **Show all** brings hidden parts back. Press Esc to leave the stencil.

To change a stencil beyond its parts’ colours, words and visibility, choose **Detach from stencil** at the bottom of the section. It becomes a plain group that looks the same and no longer follows its stencil.

On iPad, double-tap instead of double-click, and the rows sit under their labels.
