What is free, and what does the purchase add?

Dayfolio is free to download and use: one journal and up to 100 entries. Within those, everything works: writing, search, export, import, sync, the lock, reminders, quick entry, people and projects, and the templates you add from the library, which you can change however you like. You can also make a report, pick its journals and fields, and read it on screen.

The purchase adds as many journals and entries as you like, pictures, PDFs and files in entries, templates of your own made from scratch, and keeping and sending reports: Save preset, PDF, Word, Markdown, Share, Print and Copy. Entries over the limit, from sync, an import or a refund, stay readable, editable and exportable. Everything you write stays yours to read, edit and export, whether or not you buy.

I bought Dayfolio on my iPhone. Why does my Mac ask me to buy it?

A purchase covers one platform: iPhone and iPad together, or Mac, on every device of that platform signed in to your Apple Account. If you own one, the other platform offers you only the upgrade, not the full price: Upgrade from iPhone & iPad on the Mac, Upgrade from Mac on iPhone and iPad. An upgrade does nothing on its own; it adds the other platform to the one you own. If you want all three from the start, buy All.

How do I restore my purchase?

Open Settings › General › Purchase and choose Restore purchases. On iPhone, Settings is under More; on iPad it is in the sidebar. The paywall has a Restore purchases button too. Use the Apple Account you bought with. If Dayfolio finds nothing, it says which platform it looked for, and the usual cause is a different Apple Account on that device. Restoring a purchase is not the same as restoring a backup; for that, see Back up and restore.

How do refunds work?

Not what you need? Apple handles refunds at reportaproblem.apple.com. Apple processes every purchase and decides every refund, so we cannot issue one ourselves, but do tell us what went wrong.

How do I sync between my devices?

Sync uses your own iCloud, and it is on by default when your device is signed in to iCloud. Sign in to the same Apple Account on your other iPhone, iPad or Mac and open Dayfolio: on first launch it looks for your journals in iCloud and brings them over. Settings › Sync shows the status, and turns sync on or off.

Everything you write or choose syncs, including templates, reminders and reports. A few settings stay on each device, such as Open at login, the menu bar icon and the quick-entry shortcut on the Mac. Reminders fire on every device. If you edit the same text on two devices, Dayfolio keeps both: the other version appears as a "conflicted copy" entry in the same journal, named after the device that wrote it. Turning sync off does not remove what is already in iCloud; Delete from iCloud… in Settings › Sync does.

On iPhone and iPad, what a widget, the Share sheet, Siri or a reminder reply wrote is sent by the app: at once while it is open, and otherwise in the background, within hours. Opening Dayfolio sends it straight away.

How do I lock a journal?

Open Journal settings › Privacy and turn on Lock with Face ID (Touch ID on a device that has it). A Mac without Touch ID unlocks with your login password, and iPhone and iPad fall back to your passcode. The journal's entries are hidden until you unlock it, and they stay out of the timeline, search, widgets, notifications and Spotlight while it is locked. You choose how soon journals lock again in Settings › Locked journals. The lock hides a journal inside Dayfolio; files you export are not encrypted.

How do I back up my journal, and restore it?

On the Mac choose File › Export…; on iPhone and iPad choose More › Export…. Pick this journal or Everything, and choose Dayfolio archive. The archive is a single file that holds your journals, entries, templates, attachments, Recently deleted and the settings that sync, and you can keep it wherever you like. On iPhone and iPad Dayfolio writes it straight into a folder you pick in Files. It is not encrypted, so store it somewhere you trust.

To restore, choose File › Import… (More › Import… on iPhone and iPad), or open a .dayfolio file from Files, Mail or AirDrop, and pick the archive. Restoring adds only what is missing and never changes or removes anything already on the device. The settings in the archive apply only when the device has no journals yet.

If you would rather have plain files, choose Markdown folder when you export. Each entry becomes one Markdown file, with its attachments alongside. A Markdown export is readable anywhere, but it cannot be restored into Dayfolio as it is; a Dayfolio archive can.

The archive's format is public. The Dayfolio archive format describes every file and key precisely enough to write your own reader.

Can I import Markdown files?

Yes. Choose File › Import… (More › Import… on iPhone and iPad) and pick a folder of Markdown files. Each file becomes an entry in a new journal named after the folder, and subfolders become their own journals. Entries are dated from the file's front matter, its file name or the file's own date, in that order. Titles and tags carry across, and local files the Markdown links to become attachments. Importing from Day One or Diarly is not part of this version.

Can I keep a Markdown copy of my journals in a folder? (Mac)

Yes. In Settings › Export, choose Mirror to a folder, then Choose folder… and tick the journals to mirror, such as for a notes vault or a Git repository. Dayfolio writes each entry as a Markdown file in that folder, with the same files and layout as a Markdown folder export, and keeps them current while it runs: a file is rewritten when its entry changes, moved when the title, date or journal changes, and removed when the entry is deleted or leaves a ticked journal.

A few things to know. Mirroring is one way: a change you make to a mirrored file is replaced the next time its entry changes, and is never read back. Dayfolio touches only files it wrote, which it tracks in a list kept inside the app, not in your folder, and a file whose name Dayfolio did not write is never replaced. A journal you lock is never mirrored: it is listed but cannot be ticked, and locking a mirrored journal removes the files Dayfolio wrote for it. Stop mirroring, or unticking a journal, leaves the files where they are; they are yours. The files are ordinary, unencrypted Markdown, so keep the folder somewhere you trust. Mirroring is free.

Writing notes from links (Mac)

On a Mac, a link can write a note into Dayfolio without opening a window: from a terminal, a script or a git hook. It is off until you turn it on, and only links that carry your Mac's key are written.

Turn it on in Dayfolio's Settings › General › Quick entry › Write notes from links. Dayfolio makes a key for this Mac and shows it, with Copy, and a line for your shell profile with your key already in it. The key is kept in this Mac's Keychain. It never syncs, and every Mac has its own.

Add the line to your shell profile (~/.zshrc). Settings shows it with your key in place of KEY; Copy copies it:

note() { open -g "dayfolio://note?key=KEY&text=$(osascript -l JavaScript -e 'function run(a){return encodeURIComponent(a.join(" "))}' -- "$@")"; }

Open a new terminal window, and then

note Fixed the flaky CI job

writes "Fixed the flaky CI job" as a note. Dayfolio stays in the background.

From a git hook. A hook does not read your profile, so it carries the line itself. Save this as .git/hooks/post-commit in a repository, with your key in place of KEY, and make it executable (chmod +x .git/hooks/post-commit):

#!/bin/zsh
note() { open -g "dayfolio://note?key=KEY&text=$(osascript -l JavaScript -e 'function run(a){return encodeURIComponent(a.join(" "))}' -- "$@")"; }
note "Committed: $(git log -1 --pretty=%s)"

Where the note goes. Into quick entry's journal: the one quick entry last wrote to, or your first journal. A link never writes into a journal you lock with Touch ID; while quick entry's journal is locked, links are refused rather than sent to another journal.

What you will see. Nothing, when the note is written: it is in Today so far the next time you open quick entry (Option-Command-J). When a link cannot write, because of a wrong or old key, the switch turned off, a locked journal, or the free version's 100 entries used, nothing is written and one notification says why. It never names a journal or repeats what the link carried.

Limits. A note can be up to 10,000 characters; a longer one is cut there, saved, and a notification says so. Notes from links count toward the free version's entries, like any other entry.

If someone else gets your key, choose Make a new key… in the same place. Every line and hook with the old key stops working, so copy the new line into them. Turning Write notes from links off keeps your key, so your lines work again when you turn it back on.

How do I turn location and weather on or off?

It is set per journal, and it is off for a new journal. Open Journal settings › Privacy and use Capture location & weather with new entries. With it off, new entries in that journal carry neither. With it on, your device asks for location permission the first time you create an entry; you can review that in Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services (System Settings on a Mac). To remove the place and weather from one entry, use the location details on that entry. Weather is recorded when you create an entry and is not refreshed later. When no location can be found, the row says "No location". The privacy policy explains what is sent to Apple.

Why does Dayfolio ask for my calendar?

Only to fill a meeting's time and attendees into your notes. The first time you choose Fill from calendar…, or turn on Suggest notes after meetings, your device asks for access. Dayfolio reads your calendar on the device and never sends it anywhere; an entry keeps only the event's identifier and start, so it can find the meeting again. If you refuse, everything else works, and you can change your mind in your device's privacy settings.

Why does Dayfolio ask for the camera and microphone? (iPhone and iPad)

The camera is used only when you choose to take a photo or video, or scan a document, for an entry, and the microphone only to record sound with a video. Nothing is saved to your Photos library, and Dayfolio never asks for access to it: when you add a picture you already have, the system's picker gives Dayfolio only the pictures you choose. A video is limited to 1080p and 45 seconds so that it fits the 50 MB limit on a file. A photo keeps the information stored inside it, such as where it was taken; the privacy policy says more.

How do I use Siri, Shortcuts and the Action Button?

Dayfolio adds these actions to Shortcuts, Siri and Spotlight: New entry and Quick note on iPhone and iPad, and Log in Dayfolio, Make this week's report and Get today's focus time on every platform. Log in Dayfolio asks for the words, spoken or typed, fills the template's fields from them on your device, and shows what it filled before anything is written. You can give any of them to the Action Button in your device's Settings. A journal you lock is never offered or named, and Get today's focus time adds up only journals you don't lock.

Which widgets and controls are there? (iPhone and iPad)

Add them from the Home Screen's edit mode, or in Control Center. New entry starts an entry and shows today's count, on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen, and Journals puts a button for up to three of your journals on the Home Screen. Three Home Screen widgets act on today's entry: Rate today, Today's timer and Today's checklist, each set to a journal that cannot be locked. Control Center has New entry and Dayfolio timer. A running timer also shows as a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. Nothing from a journal you lock appears in any of them.

How do I save something from another app? (iPhone and iPad)

Choose Dayfolio in any app's share sheet, which opens a sheet called Save to Dayfolio. Pick the journal and a template, add to the text if you like, and Save writes one entry with the text, the page's link and any files. A journal you lock is listed with a lock and takes the entry without opening. On the free version files are left out and Save text only is offered, since attachments come with the purchase.

Can I find entries in Spotlight? (iPhone and iPad)

Yes, once you turn it on. Show entries in Spotlight in Settings › Privacy is off until you do. It adds each entry's title and the text of its text, rich-text and checklist fields to Spotlight on that device only, for journals you don't lock. Locking a journal takes its entries out, and turning the switch off removes everything.

Does Handoff work?

Yes, between your iPhone, iPad and Mac signed in to the same Apple Account, with Handoff turned on in the system settings. Dayfolio hands over only which entry is open, and the other device opens it from your own iCloud, showing "Waiting for iCloud…" until it arrives. An entry of a journal you lock is never handed off.

My reminders do not appear

Reminders are notifications from your device, so the system has to allow them. Open Settings in Dayfolio to see whether permission is on, and check your device's Notifications settings. A reminder is skipped on a day when the journal already has an entry from that template, archived journals send none, and a Focus can hold them back: a Focus can carry the Dayfolio reminders filter, which chooses the journals whose reminders come through, and whether those of journals you lock do too.

You can answer a reminder without opening Dayfolio. For a journal you don't lock, touch and hold the notification (expand it on the Mac) to set up to three quick fields, a rating, a choice and a duration, and choose Save, or type a reply. A journal you lock gets Write now only.

How do reports work, and what is free?

Open Reports (More › Reports on iPhone, the sidebar on iPad and Mac), choose + and a kind: Weekly, Monthly, Performance review, Director update, Year-end self-review or Custom. Pick journals and fields, choose Generate and read the report on screen. That is free. Keeping a report with Save preset, and sending one, as PDF, Word, Markdown, Share, Print, Copy or CSV, come with the purchase.

You can narrow a report to one project, person or tag, group it by person, and add a For line and a Prepared by line. To add up hours for a client, give the project an hourly rate (Hourly rate… in the project's menu, or in Settings › People & projects), tick a duration or time range field, and choose Split time by in the report's settings. The report's time table then shows an amount per project, with totals per currency and never converted, and exports as CSV with one row per entry and project.

Why don't I see the AI highlights in reports?

Highlights are written on your device by Apple Intelligence, so they appear only on an iPhone, iPad or Mac that supports it, with Apple Intelligence turned on in Settings. Elsewhere the option is hidden and reports work as usual. Nothing is sent to us either way.

How do I move to a new device?

The easiest way is iCloud: sign in to your Apple Account on the new device, install Dayfolio and let it look for your journals. If you do not use sync, export a Dayfolio archive on the old device, copy it across and import it on the new one, as described above. Purchases come with your Apple Account; use Restore purchases if the new device does not show them.

What do I need to run Dayfolio?

iOS or iPadOS 26 or later on an iPhone or iPad, or macOS 26 or later on a Mac, Apple silicon or Intel. The optional report highlights, and the on-device model behind Log in Dayfolio, need a device with Apple Intelligence. Dayfolio is in English.
