How do I sync between my Macs?

Sync uses your own iCloud, and it is on by default when your Mac is signed in to iCloud. Sign in to the same Apple Account on your other Mac and open Dayfolio: on first launch it looks for your journals in iCloud and brings them over, and the footer of the sidebar shows the sync status. You can turn sync on or off in Settings › Sync.

Everything you write or choose syncs, including templates, reminders and reports. A few settings stay on each Mac, such as Open at login, the menu bar icon and the quick-entry shortcut, and reminders fire on every Mac. If you edit the same text on two Macs, Dayfolio keeps both: the other version appears as a "conflicted copy" entry in the same journal. Turning sync off does not remove what is already in iCloud; Delete from iCloud… in Settings › Sync does.

How do I lock a journal?

Open Journal settings › Privacy and turn on Lock with Touch ID. The journal's entries are hidden until you unlock it, and they stay out of the timeline and search while it is locked. A Mac without Touch ID unlocks with your login password. You can choose how soon journals lock again in Settings › Privacy. The lock hides a journal inside Dayfolio; files you export are not encrypted.

How do I back up my journal, and restore it?

Choose File › Export…, pick this journal or Everything, and choose Dayfolio archive. The archive is a single file that holds your journals, entries, templates, attachments and settings, and you can keep it wherever you like. It is not encrypted, so store it somewhere you trust.

To restore, choose File › Import… and pick the archive. Restoring adds only what is missing and never changes or removes anything already on your Mac. The settings in the archive apply only when the Mac has no journals yet. On a fresh Mac, the welcome screen has an import button too.

If you would rather have plain files, choose Markdown folder when you export. Each entry becomes one Markdown file, with its attachments alongside. Reports can be saved as Markdown, PDF or Word, or printed.

Can I import Markdown files?

Yes. Choose File › Import… and pick a folder of Markdown files. Each file becomes an entry in a new journal named after the folder, and subfolders become their own journals. Entries are dated from the file's front matter, its file name or the file's own date, in that order. Titles and tags carry across, and local files the Markdown links to become attachments. Importing from Day One or Diarly is not part of this version.

How do I turn location and weather on or off?

It is set per journal. Open Journal settings › Privacy and use Capture location & weather with new entries. With it off, new entries in that journal carry neither. With it on, macOS asks for location permission the first time you create an entry; you can review that in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services. To remove the place and weather from one entry, use the location details on that entry. Weather is recorded when you create an entry and is not refreshed later. The privacy policy explains what is sent to Apple.

Why don't I see the AI highlights in reports?

Highlights are written on your Mac by Apple Intelligence, so they appear only on a Mac that supports it, with Apple Intelligence turned on in System Settings. On any other Mac the option is hidden and reports work as usual. Nothing is sent to us either way.

How do I move to a new Mac?

The easiest way is iCloud: sign in to your Apple Account on the new Mac, install Dayfolio and let it look for your journals. If you do not use sync, export a Dayfolio archive on the old Mac, copy it across and import it on the new one, as described above.

My reminders do not appear

Reminders are notifications from your Mac, so macOS has to allow them. Open Settings › Reminders in Dayfolio to see whether permission is on, and check System Settings › Notifications. A reminder is skipped on a day when the journal already has an entry from that template, and archived journals send none.

What do I need to run Dayfolio?

macOS 26 or later, on an Apple silicon or Intel Mac. The optional report highlights need a Mac with Apple Intelligence.

How do refunds work?

Apple processes every purchase and Apple decides refunds. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com. We genuinely cannot issue one ourselves, but do tell us what went wrong.
