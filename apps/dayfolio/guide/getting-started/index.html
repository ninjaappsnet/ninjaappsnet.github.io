Dayfolio/Guide

Getting started with Dayfolio

Dayfolio is a work journal. You keep a journal for each kind of page you write, and every entry in it starts from the journal’s form, shaped like that work: a standup, a client meeting, a 1:1, a shift debrief. This page takes you from a fresh install to your first entry, and shows where everything else lives.

Your first page

The first time you open Dayfolio, it doesn’t ask you to configure anything. It shows today’s page with one question: “What are you working on today?”

  1. Write a line about your day, the way you’d say it to a colleague: “Reviewing the payments retry design and two code reviews.”
  2. After a few words, Sounds like offers up to three kinds of work that match what you wrote, always ending with General. Pick the one closest to your job. Dayfolio works this out on your device; your words go nowhere.
  3. Under the page, a line names what will be made, such as “Creates Daily standup”, the template that people in that kind of work write most, and its fields show faintly so you can see its shape. “Other templates for this kind of work are in New journal” sits under it.
  4. Choose Keep this page (⌘⏎ on a Mac or an iPad with a keyboard). Dayfolio makes a journal from that template, named for it, and your first entry, dated now, holding the line you wrote.

Nothing is created before you choose Keep this page. If you pick General, your words become a note in Notes, the journal that’s always there, and you make journals from the General templates later, in New journal.

After Keep, a card under the entry asks “Write again tomorrow?” with the time the template suggests, for example weekdays at 09:00 for a standup or 17:30 for a billable day. Remind me adds that reminder to the journal; Not now adds nothing. If the journal is for notes about people, such as one made for management, Dayfolio also asks once whether to lock it. See Locked journals and privacy.

Other ways to start

  • Already have journals? Import… restores a Dayfolio archive or a Markdown folder. See Import, export and backups.
  • If you already use Dayfolio on another device with iCloud, a new device looks for your journals in iCloud first and opens them, with no setup at all. See Sync and iCloud.

Journals, templates and entries

  • A journal holds the entries for one kind of page you write, such as Engineering Log or Fernhill Client. It has a name, a colour and an icon, and exactly one template, its form, which no other journal shares.
  • A template is the shape of an entry: an ordered set of fields such as Energy, Focus time, Status, Blockers and Notes. Dayfolio’s library has 75 templates in 16 packs, one pack for each kind of work. You pick one in New journal and it becomes a journal of its own, and you can change its form. See Journals, folders and goals, Journals, forms and fields and the template gallery.
  • Notes is a journal that’s always there, for everything that has no form of its own.
  • An entry is one page in a journal, made from its form at a date and time. A journal can have any number of entries on the same day.

Write today’s entry

Today opens your current journal at today’s most recent entry. When there’s nothing yet, it says “Nothing written today” and offers to start the journal’s form in one tap, for example Start today’s standup. If you keep several journals, Settings › General › Today can show a set of them together. See Journals, folders and goals.

To start any other entry:

  • On a Mac: press ⌘N, or choose File › New entry. The entry is made at once, from the current journal’s form, and opens. To choose another journal or a different date first, press ⌥⌘N (or hold ⌥ and click New entry): the New entry sheet lists your journals, with a preview of each form, and you choose Create entry. ⌘0 in the sheet writes a Blank entry into Notes.
  • On iPhone: tap the round New entry button above the tab bar. It writes the current journal’s form at once. Touch and hold it for a menu: New entry in this journal, Blank entry in Notes, your recent journals and Choose journal and date….
  • On iPad: use New entry in the list column’s bar; touch and hold it for the same menu.

Fill in the fields and write. Dayfolio saves as you type, and “Saved” shows in the toolbar; there’s no Save button. Past entries stay editable.

Add another journal

Open New journal: on a Mac, choose + beside Journals in the sidebar, or File › New journal…; on iPhone and iPad, go to More › Journals and tap +. Choose a template, from Suggested for you at the top or from the library by pack, or choose Blank journal. The journal takes the template’s name, icon and colour, and you can change them. Create makes it. Make the same template again for a second client and the new journal is numbered, “Client meeting 2”.

You can group journals in folders, one level deep, and choose which journals Today shows. See Journals, folders and goals.

Make it yours

For your first week, once you’ve kept your first page, Today shows a small card, “Make it yours”, that starts at “1 of 4”: your first entry, a reminder, another journal and a folder. Each step ticks when it’s true, and each can be skipped or the whole card hidden. It never nags, and it never counts a skipped step as done.

Location and weather

Dayfolio can record where you wrote an entry and the weather there at the time. A new journal starts with this off. To turn it on, open the journal’s settings and switch on Capture location & weather with new entries under Privacy. Weather is fetched once, when the entry is made, and never changed afterwards.

Tips

  • On a Mac or an iPad with a keyboard, press ⌘J for Go to… and type a day (“fri”, “3 oct”), a journal or a template.
  • On a Mac or an iPad with a keyboard, press Tab after an entry’s title to fill its fields in one line, such as “energy 4, focus 2h, status on track”. See Journals, forms and fields.
  • Star the entries you’re proud of as you go. Reports put them first. See Reports.
  • Delete something by mistake? The message that says “Moved to Recently deleted” has Undo.
