How Dayfolio works

Each page of the guide takes one part of Dayfolio, says what it’s for and walks you through it, on iPhone, iPad and Mac. If you’re new, start with the first page; otherwise go straight to the part you need. Questions about purchases, sync and privacy are answered on the support page.

01

Getting started with Dayfolio

Write one line about your work and Dayfolio sets up a journal with the right templates. Your first page, journals, templates and new entries.

→ 02

Templates and fields

Pick templates from Dayfolio’s library of 75, change their fields, and fill an entry fast with the Fill line, last time’s values and a running timer.

→ 03

Carry-over between entries

Dayfolio carries open action items, the client and the project into your next entry, following each person and client. Here’s how it works.

→ 04

Reports: weekly, director update and self-review

Turn your entries into a weekly report, a director update, a performance review or a year-end self-review, with filters, billable time and CSV.

→ 05

People and projects

Keep track of the people and projects in your notes: see everything with one name, what’s still open, and rename or merge names in Dayfolio.

→ 06

Locked journals and privacy

Lock a journal with Face ID or Touch ID and its entries stay hidden everywhere until you unlock. What the lock hides, and Dayfolio’s privacy settings.

→ 07

Quick capture

Write into Dayfolio without opening it: Mac quick entry with ⌥⌘J, notes from a terminal, drag and drop, answering a reminder, and the Share sheet.

→ 08

Siri, Shortcuts, widgets, Control Center and Focus

Log entries with Siri, rate your day from a widget, time work from Control Center and quiet reminders with Focus. Dayfolio’s system features.

→ 09

Sync and iCloud

Dayfolio syncs your journals between iPhone, iPad and Mac through your own iCloud, encrypted, and end to end with Advanced Data Protection.

→ 10

Import, export and backups

Export journals as Markdown or a Dayfolio archive, restore a backup, import a Markdown folder, and keep a live Markdown copy in a folder on your Mac.
