Privacy Policy

VERSION 1.0 — EFFECTIVE 2026-09-29

The short version: Dayfolio collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and nothing you write reaches us. Your journal lives on your Mac and, if you leave sync on, in your own private iCloud. The only other network connections the app makes are to Apple, for the place name and weather on an entry and for iCloud sync.

Dayfolio is made by NinjaApps, a trading name of cetin.info — the sole-trader business of Ahmet Cetin, operating from Poland. For the purposes of the GDPR, that business would be the data controller for any personal data the app processed. Dayfolio gives us none.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no email addresses, no passwords, no profiles.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers. Dayfolio contains no advertising SDK.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking, across this app or any other.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect anything. Everything the app talks to belongs to Apple.
  • No servers of ours. There is nowhere for your journal to be sent, so we receive none of it: not your entries, not your journal names, not your location.

Where your journal lives

Your journals, entries, templates, tags, people, projects, attachments, reminders, saved searches, reports and settings are stored on your Mac, in the app's own storage. They may be included in your Mac's backups, such as Time Machine, the same way any app's data is. We cannot see any of it.

Deleted journals and entries stay in Recently deleted for 30 days and are then removed. Deleting the app does not by itself remove data you have synced to iCloud; see the next section.

iCloud sync

If your Mac is signed in to iCloud, sync is on by default. You can turn it off at any time in Settings › Sync. With sync on, Dayfolio keeps your journal in your own private iCloud database, using Apple's CloudKit, so it appears on your other Macs. That database belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it, and it never passes through anything we operate.

The content fields of your entries are stored as CloudKit encrypted fields, and attachments are stored as CloudKit assets. Apple encrypts your data in transit and on its servers. If you have Advanced Data Protection turned on for your Apple Account, that content is end-to-end encrypted, so only your Apple devices can read it. Without Advanced Data Protection, it is encrypted with keys that Apple holds. Apple's privacy policy and iCloud terms govern that storage.

Turning sync off keeps what is already in iCloud. To remove the iCloud copy, use Delete from iCloud… in Settings › Sync.

Location and weather

Location and weather are optional and set per journal, with the switch Capture location & weather with new entries in Journal settings › Privacy. When a journal has it on, Dayfolio does the following each time you create an entry in it:

  • macOS asks for your permission the first time, and Dayfolio asks only for location while the app is in use. You can change this in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services.
  • Your Mac's location is sent to Apple, to look up the place name with MapKit reverse geocoding and the weather with WeatherKit. If you choose another place by searching for an address, the text you search for goes to Apple's MapKit as well. No third-party service sees your location.
  • What comes back is stored in that entry only: the street and city (no house number), coordinates rounded to about 100 metres, and a snapshot of the weather at that time. Weather is not fetched again for old entries.
  • If your Mac is offline at that moment, Dayfolio retries for up to seven days and stores the result in the same entry.
  • None of this is sent to us. With sync on, it travels with the entry into your own iCloud, like the rest of the entry.

With the switch off, entries in that journal have no location and no weather. You can also remove the location and weather from any single entry. If you name a place, for example "Warsaw office", Dayfolio remembers that name on your Mac, and later entries near it show it; you can rename or delete these places in Settings › Privacy.

Apple Weather

Weather information in Dayfolio is provided by Apple Weather. Apple requires this attribution, and you can read about Apple Weather's data sources at weatherkit.apple.com/legal-attribution.html. Dayfolio shows the same attribution next to weather details in the app.

Locking a journal

You can lock any journal with Touch ID. Dayfolio asks macOS to check who you are, using Apple's LocalAuthentication framework. macOS does the checking and answers only yes or no, or asks for your Mac login password when there is no Touch ID. Your fingerprint data is held by macOS and is never accessible to Dayfolio, and we never see it. The only thing Dayfolio stores is whether a journal is locked, alongside the journal itself.

Highlights in reports

Reports can include a few optional highlights, written by Apple's on-device model through the Foundation Models framework. It is available only on Macs with Apple Intelligence, and it runs on your Mac: the text of your report is handled on the device, and it is not sent to us or to any server of ours. On other Macs the option does not appear. You can edit the highlights, or leave them off.

Reminders

Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your Mac, and only after you set one up. macOS asks for permission to send notifications the first time. There are no push notifications and no server involved: nothing is sent from anywhere.

Exports, backups and imports

You can export entries as a Markdown folder or as a Dayfolio archive, save a report as a PDF or a Word document, or print it. These files are written only where you choose to put them, and they are ordinary files: Dayfolio does not encrypt them, and a Markdown export in particular is plain text. Anyone who can open the file can read it, so keep them somewhere you trust. If you share an entry through the macOS share sheet, you choose where it goes.

Importing reads only the archive or folder you pick, on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded.

Apple services the app talks to

  • iCloud (CloudKit) — for sync, only if you are signed in and leave it on, as described above.
  • MapKit and WeatherKit — for the place name and weather, only in journals where you leave location and weather on.
  • LocalAuthentication — for unlocking a locked journal, on your Mac.
  • Foundation Models — for the optional report highlights, on your Mac.
  • The Mac App Store — Apple processes your purchase. We never see your payment details.

Our website

The app does not connect to this website. The website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub logs visitor IP addresses for security under GitHub's privacy statement, and we have no access to those logs and keep none of our own.

Children

Dayfolio is a work journal and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children, and it has no chat, no user-generated content shared with others and no web browsing.

Your choices, and deleting your data

  • Location and weather — switch it off for a journal in Journal settings › Privacy, or take away the app's location permission in System Settings.
  • Sync — turn it off in Settings › Sync, and remove the iCloud copy with Delete from iCloud….
  • Data on your Mac — delete journals and entries in the app, or delete the app and its data.
  • Purchases — refunds are Apple's decision, at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Your rights under the GDPR

The GDPR gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection over personal data a controller holds about you. We hold none: there is no account to look up, no identifier we could search on, and no database. Your journal is yours, on your Mac and in your own iCloud, and you can already read, change, export and delete it there.

You can raise a concern with us at support@ninjaapps.net, or lodge a complaint with the Polish supervisory authority, the Urząd Ochrony Danych Osobowych (UODO), at uodo.gov.pl.
