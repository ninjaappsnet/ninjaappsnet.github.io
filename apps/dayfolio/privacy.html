Privacy Policy

VERSION 1.1 — EFFECTIVE 2026-10-15

The short version: Dayfolio collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and nothing you write reaches us. Your journal lives on your iPhone, iPad or Mac and, if you leave sync on, in your own private iCloud. The only other network connections the app makes are to Apple: for the place name and weather on an entry, for iCloud sync, and for purchases.

This policy covers Dayfolio for iPhone, iPad and Mac, which share one App Store listing and the same data practices. Where the apps differ, it says so. Version 1.1 replaces version 1.0, which described the Mac app only; the version history is at the end.

Dayfolio is made by NinjaApps, a trading name of cetin.info — the sole-trader business of Ahmet Cetin, operating from Poland. For the purposes of the GDPR, that business would be the data controller for any personal data the app processed. Dayfolio gives us none.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no email addresses, no passwords, no profiles.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers. Dayfolio contains no advertising SDK.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking, across this app or any other.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect anything. Everything the app talks to belongs to Apple.
  • No servers of ours. There is nowhere for your journal to be sent, so we receive none of it: not your entries, not your journal names, not your location, not your calendar.

Where your journal lives

Your journals, entries, templates, tags, people, projects, attachments, reminders, saved searches, reports and settings are stored on your device, in the app's own storage. On iPhone and iPad that storage is an App Group container, which Dayfolio shares only with its own widgets, controls and extensions so that they can read and write the same journal on your device. On the Mac, Dayfolio's reminder notification extension does the same. Nothing in them sends anything to us. Stored data may be included in your device's backups, such as iCloud Backup or Time Machine, the same way any app's data is. We cannot see any of it.

Deleted journals and entries stay in Recently deleted for 30 days and are then removed. Deleting the app does not by itself remove data you have synced to iCloud; see the next section.

iCloud sync and Advanced Data Protection

If your device is signed in to iCloud, sync is on by default. You can turn it off at any time in Settings › Sync. With sync on, Dayfolio keeps your journal in your own private iCloud database, using Apple's CloudKit, so it appears on your other iPhones, iPads and Macs. That database belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it, and it never passes through anything we operate.

The content fields of your entries are stored as CloudKit encrypted fields, and attachments are stored as CloudKit assets. Apple encrypts your data in transit and on its servers. If you have Advanced Data Protection turned on for your Apple Account, that content is end-to-end encrypted, so only your Apple devices can read it. Without Advanced Data Protection, it is encrypted with keys that Apple holds. Apple's privacy policy and iCloud terms govern that storage.

Apple's push service tells your devices when something has changed so that Dayfolio can fetch it, in the background too on iPhone and iPad. That is Apple talking to your devices; no server of ours is involved.

Turning sync off keeps what is already in iCloud. To remove the iCloud copy, use Delete from iCloud… in Settings › Sync.

Permissions and system features

Dayfolio asks for each of these only when you first use the feature that needs it. You can refuse any of them, or turn them off later in your device's Settings (System Settings on a Mac), and the rest of the app keeps working. None of what follows is sent to us.

Location (iPhone, iPad and Mac)

Location is optional and set per journal, with the switch Capture location & weather with new entries in Journal settings › Privacy. It is off for a new journal. When a journal has it on, Dayfolio does the following each time you create an entry in it:

  • Your device asks for your permission the first time, and Dayfolio asks only for location while the app is in use, never in the background.
  • Your device's location is sent to Apple, to look up the place name with MapKit reverse geocoding and the weather with WeatherKit. If you choose another place by searching for an address, the text you search for goes to Apple's MapKit as well. No third-party service sees your location.
  • What comes back is stored in that entry only: the street and city (no house number), coordinates rounded to about 100 metres, and a snapshot of the weather at that time. Weather is not fetched again for old entries.
  • If your device is offline at that moment, Dayfolio retries for up to seven days and stores the result in the same entry.
  • None of this is sent to us. With sync on, it travels with the entry into your own iCloud, like the rest of the entry.

With the switch off, entries in that journal have no location and no weather. You can also remove the location and weather from any single entry. If you name a place, for example "Warsaw office", Dayfolio remembers that name on your device, and later entries near it show it; you can rename or delete these places in Settings › Privacy.

Weather

Weather information in Dayfolio is provided by Apple Weather. Apple requires this attribution, and you can read about Apple Weather's data sources at weatherkit.apple.com/legal-attribution.html. Dayfolio shows the same attribution next to weather details in the app and ends a Markdown export that holds weather with a link to it.

Calendar

Dayfolio asks for access to your calendar the first time you choose Fill from calendar…, or when you turn on Suggest notes after meetings. It reads your events on your device, to fill a meeting's time and attendees into the notes you write about it and to suggest writing notes after a meeting. Your calendar is never sent to us or to any server. An entry written from a meeting keeps only the event's identifier and the start of that occurrence, so it can find the meeting again; with sync on, that travels with the entry into your own iCloud. If you refuse or take back access, everything else keeps working.

Camera (iPhone and iPad)

Dayfolio uses the camera only when you choose to take a photo or video, or scan a document, for an entry. The picture, video or scan goes into that entry as an attachment, and nothing is saved to your Photos library. A photo or video taken in Dayfolio carries where it was taken only when its journal's Capture location & weather switch is on and you have allowed location. Dayfolio does not ask for access to your photo library: when you add a picture you already have, the system's picker hands Dayfolio only the pictures you choose.

Microphone (iPhone and iPad)

Dayfolio uses the microphone only to record sound with a video you take for an entry. It does not record audio in any other way.

Photos, files and what is inside them

Pictures, videos, PDFs and other files that you add, choose, paste or share into Dayfolio are kept exactly as they are, up to 50 MB each. That includes whatever information is stored inside a file, such as the place a photo was taken or the camera that took it. Dayfolio does not remove it, and it goes wherever the file goes: into your iCloud when sync is on, into exports and archives, and into anything you share. To search inside attachments, Dayfolio reads the text in images and PDFs on your device; that text is not sent anywhere.

Notifications

Reminders are local notifications that your device schedules itself, and only after you set one up. Your device asks for permission to send notifications the first time a reminder is switched on. There is no push server of ours: nothing is sent from anywhere. A reminder for a journal you lock names neither the journal nor anything in it; it only says that it is time to write. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, a reminder for a journal you don't lock can take a reply or quick fields (a rating, a choice, a duration), which are written into your journal on your device. The Mac can also post a notification when a quick-note link could not write a note, saying why without quoting the note.

Face ID, Touch ID and the lock

You can lock any journal. Dayfolio asks the system to check who you are, using Apple's LocalAuthentication framework: Face ID or Touch ID, with your device passcode, or your Mac's login password, as the fallback. The system does the checking and answers only yes or no. Your face and fingerprint data are held by the system, are never accessible to Dayfolio, and we never see them. The only thing Dayfolio stores is whether a journal is locked, alongside the journal itself. A journal you lock stays out of widgets, notifications, Shortcuts lists, Handoff, Spotlight and the app switcher.

Siri and Shortcuts

Dayfolio offers actions to Siri and Shortcuts: New entry and Quick note on iPhone and iPad, and Log in Dayfolio, Make this week's report and Get today's focus time on every platform. They run on your device. When you use Log in Dayfolio by voice, Siri turns your speech into words under Apple's terms, and Dayfolio receives only the words. It then fills the template's fields from them, with Apple's on-device model where one is available and with simple rules otherwise, shows you what it filled, and writes nothing until you confirm. The journals offered to Siri and Shortcuts never include a journal you lock, and Get today's focus time adds up only journals you don't lock. How Siri and Shortcuts treat what you say and the results is covered by Apple's privacy policy; none of it comes to us.

Spotlight (iPhone and iPad)

Spotlight is off until you turn on Show entries in Spotlight in Settings › Privacy. Then Dayfolio gives the system's Spotlight index, on that device only, each entry's title and the text of its text, rich-text and checklist fields, for journals you don't lock. That is a second copy of that text, kept by the system outside Dayfolio's own storage. Locking a journal removes its entries from the index, and turning the switch off removes everything Dayfolio added. The index stays on your device: it is not synced and not sent to us.

Widgets, controls and Live Activities (iPhone and iPad)

Dayfolio's widgets and Control Center controls read the same database on your device. They show things such as today's entry count, a rating, a checklist or the running timer, and they let you start an entry, rate, tick an item or start a timer. The interactive widgets work on a journal you choose from those that can't be locked, and no widget, control or Live Activity shows anything from a journal you lock, not even its name or a count. The timer's Live Activity shows the journal's colour, the template and field names and the time on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; Dayfolio starts, updates and ends it on your device. Where the system hides private content on a locked screen, Dayfolio's values are hidden too.

The Share extension (iPhone and iPad)

Dayfolio appears in other apps' share sheets and opens a sheet called Save to Dayfolio. When you use it, the extension receives only what you share: text, a page's link and files. It writes one entry into your journal on your device. It does not read anything else from the app you came from, and it never reads your entries; a journal you lock is listed with a lock and takes the entry without opening.

Handoff

Handoff lets you pick up the entry you have open on another iPhone, iPad or Mac signed in to your Apple Account. While an entry is open, Dayfolio tells the system only which entry it is, by its identifier and nothing of its content, and the other device opens it from your own iCloud. Dayfolio never hands off an entry of a journal you lock, and stops when you close the entry, the journal relocks or it becomes lockable. Handoff is Apple's service, and you can turn it off in your device's settings.

Focus

Dayfolio offers a Focus filter, Dayfolio reminders. When you add it to a Focus, you choose which journals' reminders may come through while that Focus is on, and whether reminders of journals you lock may too. Dayfolio sees only the choices you made in the filter, on your device, and sends nothing to us.

On-device AI and Writing Tools

Reports can include a few optional highlights, written by Apple's on-device model through the Foundation Models framework, and Log in Dayfolio can use the same model to fill a template's fields from the words you give it. The model is available only on devices with Apple Intelligence, and it runs on your device: the text it handles is not sent to us or to any server of ours. On other devices these options do not appear, or simple rules do the work. You can edit the highlights, or leave them off.

Separately, the system's Writing Tools are off in Dayfolio's entry editor by default, because some of their operations would send what you have written to Apple's servers. Keep entries out of Writing Tools in Settings › Locked journals controls this.

Exports, backups and imports

You can export entries as a Markdown folder or as a Dayfolio archive, save a report as a PDF, Word or Markdown file, save a report's time table as CSV, or print and share. These files are written only where you choose to put them, and they are ordinary files: Dayfolio does not encrypt them, and a Markdown export in particular is plain text. Anyone who can open a file can read it, so keep them somewhere you trust. On iPhone and iPad, Dayfolio writes straight into a folder you pick in Files. If you share an entry through the share sheet, you choose where it goes.

Importing reads only the archive or folder you pick, on your device. Nothing is uploaded.

The mirror folder (Mac)

On the Mac, Mirror to a folder in Settings › Export keeps a Markdown copy of the journals you choose in a folder you choose, such as a notes vault or a Git repository. That is a second, plain-text copy of those entries outside Dayfolio's own storage. It lives where you put it: Dayfolio does not encrypt it, and any app that can open the folder can read it, as can any service that syncs or backs up that folder. A journal you lock can't be chosen, and locking a mirrored journal removes the files Dayfolio wrote for it. Dayfolio keeps a list of the files it wrote, inside its own storage and not in your folder, and never touches anything else in the folder. Stopping the mirror leaves the files where they are; they are yours to keep or delete. The folder is remembered on that Mac only and is not synced.

The quick-note link (Mac)

The Mac can write a note from a link such as dayfolio://note?key=…, for a terminal alias or a git hook. It is off until you turn on Write notes from links in Settings. A link writes a note only if it carries your Mac's secret key, which Dayfolio makes when you turn the feature on and keeps in your Mac's Keychain; the key is never synced and never logged. A link without it writes nothing. The note goes into your quick-entry journal on that Mac, never a journal you lock. When a link can't write, Dayfolio posts one notification saying why, without naming a journal or quoting the text. Make a new key… cancels every old link.

Apple services the app talks to

  • iCloud (CloudKit) — for sync, only if you are signed in and leave it on, as described above.
  • MapKit and WeatherKit — for the place name and weather, only in journals where you turn location and weather on.
  • LocalAuthentication — for unlocking a locked journal, on your device.
  • EventKit — for reading your calendar on your device, only if you allow it.
  • Foundation Models — for the optional highlights and for filling fields in Log in Dayfolio, on your device.
  • Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight, Focus, Handoff and Live Activities — system features on your device, under Apple's privacy policy.
  • The App Store — Apple processes your purchase and tells the app which purchases your Apple Account holds. We never see your payment details.

Our website

The app does not connect to this website, except when you choose to open it, for example the privacy policy or support page. The website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub logs visitor IP addresses for security under GitHub's privacy statement, and we have no access to those logs and keep none of our own.

Children

Dayfolio is a work journal and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children, and it has no chat, no user-generated content shared with others and no web browsing.

Your choices, and deleting your data

  • Location and weather — switch it off for a journal in Journal settings › Privacy, or take away the app's location permission in your device's Settings.
  • Calendar, camera, microphone and notifications — refuse or take back each one in your device's Settings; the rest of the app keeps working.
  • Spotlight — turn off Show entries in Spotlight in Settings › Privacy.
  • Sync — turn it off in Settings › Sync, and remove the iCloud copy with Delete from iCloud….
  • Mirror folder and quick-note link — stop mirroring, or turn off Write notes from links, in Settings on the Mac.
  • Data on your device — delete journals and entries in the app, or delete the app and its data.
  • Purchases — refunds are Apple's decision, at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Your rights under the GDPR

The GDPR gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection over personal data a controller holds about you. We hold none: there is no account to look up, no identifier we could search on, and no database. Your journal is yours, on your devices and in your own iCloud, and you can already read, change, export and delete it there.

You can raise a concern with us at support@ninjaapps.net, or lodge a complaint with the Polish supervisory authority, the Urząd Ochrony Danych Osobowych (UODO), at uodo.gov.pl.
