Diagrove

Privacy Policy

VERSION 1.0 — EFFECTIVE 2026-11-06

The short version: Diagrove collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and nothing you draw or type reaches us. Your documents are files you keep where you choose, on your Mac or iPad or in your own iCloud Drive. The AI runs on your device. Diagrove goes on the network only when you ask it to read live data from a web address in a diagram, and then it talks to that server, not to us.

This policy covers Diagrove for Mac, Diagrove for iPad and the diagrove command-line tool for the Mac. The two apps share the same code and the same data practices; where they differ, it says so.

Diagrove is made by NinjaApps, a trading name of cetin.info — the sole-trader business of Ahmet Cetin, operating from Poland. For the purposes of the GDPR, that business would be the data controller for any personal data the app processed. Diagrove gives us none.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email addresses, no passwords, no profiles.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers. Diagrove contains no advertising SDK.
  • No analytics, no crash-reporting service and no tracking, across this app or any other. Reports of crashes and slowdowns stay on your device unless you send them to us (see Diagnostic reports).
  • No third-party SDKs. The apps are built from Apple’s frameworks and our own code.
  • No servers of ours. There is nowhere for your documents to be sent, so we receive none of them: not your diagrams, not the words in them, not what you type in the prompt bar.
  • No update checks, and nothing that calls home.
  • No permissions to ask for. Diagrove does not ask for access to your camera, microphone, photo library, location, contacts, calendar or notifications.
  • No Spotlight index and no Handoff: Diagrove adds nothing to Spotlight and offers nothing to Handoff.

The app’s privacy manifests declare no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data, and Diagrove’s App Store privacy answer is “Data Not Collected”.

Where your documents live

A Diagrove document is a .diagrove file that you save where you choose: on your Mac, on your iPad, in iCloud Drive or with another file provider. It holds your canvases and everything on them, as you made them:

  • the words in your shapes and lines, and the document’s variables;
  • the pictures you added, and a copy of every stencil the document uses;
  • bindings, with the web addresses and shortcut names they read, and any JSON file you chose to keep in the document;
  • actions, such as the links a graphic opens in Play mode;
  • a small picture of the first canvas, preview.png, which the Finder, Files and Quick Look show as its thumbnail.

A document does not record who made it or on which device. Pictures are stored under names made from their content, so a picture’s original file name is not kept. Anyone you give a document to can see all of it, including its preview, its pictures and the addresses its bindings read.

Diagrove also keeps a few things of its own in the app’s storage on your device. They are not synced, not shared with other apps and never sent to us:

  • Your templates. Each document you save with File › Save as template… is kept as a whole copy, pictures included, with its name, its line and a picture for the gallery.
  • Your libraries. The stencils you save, as .diagrovestencils files.
  • Pictures of stencils, in a cache, so the Stencils sidebar opens quickly.
  • Diagnostic reports, as described below.

On the Mac, Diagrove is sandboxed by macOS: it opens only the documents and files you choose. Stored data may be included in your device’s backups, such as iCloud Backup or Time Machine, the same way any app’s data is; diagnostic reports are left out of them. We cannot see any of it.

What Diagrove remembers on your device

Diagrove keeps its settings in its own preferences on each device. They stay on that device and are never sent anywhere. Most are choices about how the app looks:

  • the switches in Settings: Show the Welcome window when Diagrove opens, Show the prompt bar guide when Diagrove opens and New diagrams start on;
  • your favourite and recent stencils, your recent colours, which stencil libraries are folded, how the sidebar and inspector are laid out, and whether rulers and guides show;
  • which Getting started steps you have done, by name only, and whether you hid it;
  • the layout you last chose for a sketch, and, on the iPad, whether you have drawn with an Apple Pencil in Diagrove.

A few remember where your documents are:

  • The export sheet remembers the choice you made the last time you exported each document, for up to the 50 documents you exported most recently. It knows each one by its location on your device, the folder path and file name, and keeps the date of that export with it.
  • On the iPad, Diagrove remembers the last document you opened, as a bookmark to its file, so that a new diagram from a template or Describe a diagram goes beside it.
  • Recent documents. On the Mac, macOS keeps the list behind File › Open Recent, and the Welcome window reads it to show each document’s name, folder and when it last changed. On the iPad, the Recents beside your documents are the Files app’s.

iCloud Drive

Diagrove has its own folder in iCloud Drive, called Diagrove. A document you save in iCloud Drive is kept in your own Apple Account and opens on your other Macs and iPads signed in to it. On the iPad, a new diagram made from a template or with Describe a diagram goes beside the last document you opened; if there is none, it goes into iCloud Drive › Diagrove when iCloud Drive is on, and otherwise into On My iPad › Diagrove.

iCloud Drive is Apple’s service. Apple encrypts your files in transit and on its servers, and if you have Advanced Data Protection turned on, iCloud Drive is end-to-end encrypted. Diagrove doesn’t run a server of its own and has no access to your iCloud. Your templates and your libraries are not synced through iCloud in version 1.0. Apple’s privacy policy and iCloud terms govern that storage.

Live data and the network

A binding lets a graphic show a value from outside the document. Most bindings never touch the network: a document variable, a JSON file kept inside the document, or a shortcut’s output. One kind does: a binding to JSON from a web address.

That address is part of the document. It may be one you typed, or one in a document someone else made, in which case the request goes to the server they chose. Diagrove reads only https addresses, and only when you start it:

  • when you press Play, or switch on Show live data in the inspector, and then every 30 seconds while either lasts;
  • when you choose Load now in the inspector’s Data section, or Refresh live data or Retry in Play mode.

Opening a document never reads it, and the reading stops when you stop playing, switch Show live data off or close the window. A read that fails gives up after 20 seconds and keeps the last good value; Diagrove tries again on the next 30-second read or when you choose Retry.

The request goes straight from your device to that server, as a web request from any app would. We are not in between and never see it. What the server receives:

  • your device’s IP address, as with any connection;
  • the headers the system adds to every request: Diagrove’s name and build number and your system’s version (the user agent), and your preferred languages.

Nothing else. Diagrove reads live data without cookies: it sends none and keeps none a server tries to set, so the server can’t recognise your device from one read to the next by a cookie. It also keeps no cache and no stored passwords for these reads. What comes back is used for the drawing, in memory, and is not saved in the document or anywhere else on your device. What the server does with your request is up to whoever runs it, under their own privacy policy.

Diagrove opens other things only when you choose them, and hands each to the app that handles it, without fetching anything itself:

  • a graphic’s Open link action in Play mode, which opens the link in your browser or the app it belongs to;
  • a graphic’s Run shortcut action, and a binding to a shortcut, which run the shortcut you named in Apple’s Shortcuts app. Shortcut bindings run when Play mode starts, and when you refresh or run them. A shortcut can do anything it was built to do, including going on the network; that is up to the shortcut. Its output comes back to Diagrove and is kept in memory only;
  • the Help menu’s pages, the command-line tool’s download page and the licences in About Diagrove, which open in your browser;
  • the settings Diagrove points you to, such as Apple Intelligence or Automation.

Diagnostic reports

Your Mac or iPad hands Diagrove reports through Apple’s MetricKit, about a day after the time they cover: reports of crashes, hangs, heavy disk writes and CPU exceptions, and a summary of each day’s performance, such as memory and energy use. Diagrove keeps them in its own storage on the device for 30 days, up to 10 MB in all, and then deletes the oldest. They are left out of your device’s backups.

They describe how the app ran, such as where in its code a crash happened. Apple’s format also names your device’s model, your system’s version, Diagrove’s version and your region setting. They contain no documents and nothing you typed.

Diagrove never sends them. If you want help with a problem, you can choose to send them to us: on the Mac with Help › Show diagnostic reports, which shows the files in the Finder for you to attach to an email, and on the iPad with Help › Share diagnostic reports…, which opens the share sheet. What you send is used only to solve the problem you wrote about, and deleted when it is solved.

Diagrove also writes failures, such as a document that couldn’t be opened or a web address that couldn’t be read, to the system’s log on your device. The log entry says what failed; file names, paths, web addresses and the names you gave things are marked private, so the system doesn’t show them.

Permissions and system features

Diagrove asks for no permissions. It uses these system features only when you do, and none of what follows is sent to us.

Pictures, files and what is inside them

You add pictures through the system’s own choosers, which run outside the app and hand Diagrove only what you pick: the open panel on the Mac, and on the iPad the photo picker (Picture from Photos…) and the Files picker. Pictures you drag in or paste are only the ones you drag or paste. Diagrove can’t see the rest of your photo library or your files.

A picture is kept in your document exactly as it came, up to 50 MB and 100 megapixels. That includes whatever information is stored inside it, such as the place a photo was taken, the camera that took it and when. Diagrove does not remove it. It goes wherever the document goes: into iCloud Drive, into templates you save, into documents you share or send, and into some exports (see Exports and the web player). If you don’t want to share where a photo was taken, remove its location before you add it, for example in the Photos app.

Camera

Diagrove doesn’t use your device’s camera. On the Mac, File › Import from iPhone or iPad is the system’s Continuity Camera: your iPhone or iPad takes the photo or scan, and the system hands Diagrove the picture, which is then treated as any other picture.

Apple Pencil, Sketch mode and reading sketches

On the iPad, Sketch mode lets you draw over the canvas with Apple Pencil or a finger. The ink is never saved in the document: each canvas keeps its own while the window is open, and it is gone when you close it. Turn into diagram reads the ink and puts shapes and lines in its place.

On the Mac, New diagram from sketch or photo… and Turn into diagram… read a picture of a diagram drawn on paper, a whiteboard or a tablet. Only the shapes, lines and words Diagrove finds go into your document, not the picture you chose, unless it was already in it.

Both read the words in your sketch with Apple’s Vision text recognition, on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

On-device AI and Writing Tools

The prompt bar draws and changes diagrams from what you type. Diagrove reads your words with its own parser on the device and, where Apple Intelligence is on, with Apple’s on-device model through the Foundation Models framework. Both run on your Mac or iPad: your words, your diagram and the answer are not sent to us or to any server of ours. On a device without Apple Intelligence, the parser does the work. Diagrove 1.0 has no option to use an AI service in the cloud.

Separately, Diagrove leaves the system’s Writing Tools available in its text, as most apps do: the words on the canvas, the prompt bar and the inspector’s fields. Writing Tools are Apple’s, and Diagrove does not use them itself. Some of their operations can send the text you chose to Apple’s servers, through Private Cloud Compute, or, if you have turned on the ChatGPT extension in your device’s settings and choose it, to OpenAI. Nothing reaches us either way. If you want your words to stay on your device, don’t use Writing Tools on text in Diagrove.

Siri and Shortcuts

Diagrove offers two actions to Siri and Shortcuts on the Mac and the iPad. Create Diagram from Text opens Diagrove and draws a new diagram from the text it is given, on your device, as the prompt bar does; nothing is saved until you save it. Export Canvas as Image turns the first canvas of a document you give it into a PNG, PDF or SVG file and hands it back to your shortcut. When you use them by voice, Siri turns your speech into words under Apple’s terms, and Diagrove receives only the words. How Siri and Shortcuts treat what you say and the results is covered by Apple’s privacy policy; none of it comes to us.

Diagrove links

A link such as diagrove://new?text=…, from a shortcut, another app or a web page, opens a new diagram drawn from the text it carries, up to 20,000 characters. It only makes a new, unsaved document: a link can’t open, read, change or send your existing documents. Diagrove’s other links are the ones Shortcuts uses to hand a shortcut’s output back to the diagram that asked for it.

Apple Events and scripting (Mac)

On the Mac, Diagrove can be scripted with Apple Events. A script or an app you allow can read the documents open in Diagrove, including their names, where their files are and the words in them, and can change them, one undo step at a time. The diagrove command-line tool’s --live option works this way. macOS asks you before any other app controls Diagrove for the first time, and you can change your answer in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation. This happens on your Mac only.

Thumbnails

Diagrove’s thumbnail extension draws the picture the Finder, the open panels and the Files app show for a .diagrove file, from the document’s own preview.png. It runs on your device, reads only the file the system asks about, and makes no network requests.

Copy, paste and drag and drop

When you copy or cut graphics, Diagrove puts them on the system pasteboard, as Diagrove’s own data and, on the Mac, as a PDF and a PNG picture for other apps; copying Diagrove text puts plain text there. Diagrove reads the pasteboard only when you paste, and takes only what you drag onto it. With Universal Clipboard, the system can make what you copy available on your other devices signed in to your Apple Account; that is Apple’s feature, under Apple’s terms.

Share, Print and Send to Dayfolio

  • Share… hands the document to the system’s share sheet, and Print… to the system’s print panel. Where it goes from there, by AirDrop, Mail, Messages, a printer or another app, is your choice.
  • Send to Dayfolio appears only when Dayfolio, our work journal, is installed. To know, Diagrove asks the system: on the Mac whether an app with Dayfolio’s identifier is installed, and on the iPad whether Dayfolio’s link can be opened. The answer stays on your device. It hands the share sheet the document, zipped, and a picture of the current canvas, with Dayfolio first; you choose where they go. Diagrove writes the two files to its temporary folder for the share sheet, and removes them the next time you use Send to Dayfolio, once an hour has passed.

Exports and the web player

You can export a canvas as a PNG, PDF or SVG file, and, on the Mac, a web player: a folder that plays the diagram in a browser. These files are written only where you choose to put them, and they are ordinary files that anyone who can open them can read.

  • A PNG export is a new picture of the canvas, drawn by Diagrove.
  • An SVG export and a web player keep the pictures in them as they are, where browsers can show them as they are: PNG, JPEG and GIF pictures that stand upright, and in the web player WebP too, keep their own bytes, including any location or camera details inside them. Other pictures are drawn again first, which leaves those details out.
  • A web player holds the whole document, as in its .diagrove file: the words, the pictures, the links its graphics open and the web addresses its bindings read.

A web player makes no request to us. It loads only its own files, uses your system’s fonts, and has no analytics. When it plays a diagram with a binding to a web address, the browser reads that address when the page opens and every 30 seconds while the page is in view, without cookies, and the server receives what a browser sends with any request, such as your IP address. The first time the page tells someone at phone width how to pan, it remembers that it did in their browser’s storage, so it says it once. Where you put the folder, and who can see it there, is up to you.

The command-line tool

The diagrove command-line tool is a separate download for the Mac, outside the App Store. It makes no network requests and sends nothing to us.

  • It reads and writes only the files you name, on your Mac. With --app-libraries it also reads the stencil libraries the Mac app keeps, and macOS may ask you to allow it.
  • diagrove mcp is an MCP server. It talks only to the AI assistant that started it, on the same Mac, over standard input and output, and puts the pictures it draws into a temporary folder unless you name another. Whatever that assistant then sends to its own service is between you and its maker, under its privacy policy.
  • diagrove skill install writes the diagrove skill’s text files into ~/.claude/skills/diagrove, or a folder you name.
  • --live edits a document open in Diagrove through Apple Events, on the same Mac, after macOS asks you (see Apple Events and scripting).
  • Downloading it from GitHub, or installing it with Homebrew, which downloads it from GitHub, is covered by GitHub’s privacy statement and by Homebrew’s own policy, not by us.

Apple services the app talks to

  • iCloud Drive — for the documents you save there, in your own Apple Account, as described above.
  • Foundation Models — the on-device model behind the prompt bar, where Apple Intelligence is on.
  • Vision and PencilKit — reading the words in a sketch, and Sketch mode’s ink on the iPad, on your device.
  • MetricKit — the diagnostic reports kept on your device.
  • Siri, Shortcuts, Writing Tools, Continuity Camera, Universal Clipboard, the share sheet and printing — system features, used only when you use them, under Apple’s privacy policy.
  • The App Store — Apple handles Diagrove’s download, purchase and updates. We never see your payment details, and the app itself makes no purchases. Apple may collect information about downloads and, if you have chosen in your device’s settings to share analytics with app developers, about crashes and use, and make it available to us in App Store Connect. That is Apple’s collection, under Apple’s privacy policy; Diagrove contains no code that sends such reports, and we have no access to your documents through Apple.

Our website

The app does not connect to this website, except when you choose to open one of its pages, for example from the Help menu, in your browser. The website is hosted on GitHub Pages and served through Cloudflare. Both handle visitors’ IP addresses to deliver the site and keep it secure: GitHub under GitHub’s privacy statement, and Cloudflare under Cloudflare’s privacy policy. We have no access to GitHub’s logs, and we keep no logs of our own. The site has no analytics, no cookies and no trackers; it remembers only your choice of a light or dark theme, in your browser’s storage.

Children

Diagrove is a tool for drawing diagrams and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children, and it has no account, no chat and nothing shared with other people through us.

Your choices, and deleting your data

  • Live data — don’t play a diagram or show live data if you don’t want Diagrove to read its web addresses. You can see each binding’s address in the inspector’s Data section first, and remove the binding.
  • Documents, exports and libraries you exported — they are ordinary files that you can move, copy or delete.
  • Your templates and your libraries — delete them in the template gallery and the Stencils sidebar; they go to the Trash.
  • Diagnostic reports — they delete themselves after 30 days. On the Mac, Help › Show diagnostic reports shows the folder, and you can delete them at once.
  • Recent documents — on the Mac, File › Open Recent › Clear Menu.
  • Scripting — take back another app’s access in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Automation.
  • Everything else (settings, the export sheet’s memory, the reports, your templates and libraries) is in the app’s storage. On the Mac it is the folder Library/Containers/net.ninjaapps.apps.diagrove in your home folder; moving the app to the Trash may leave it behind, so delete it too if you want nothing left. On the iPad, deleting the app deletes it, and your documents stay in Files.
  • The command-line tool — delete the diagrove file, or run brew uninstall diagrove, and delete ~/.claude/skills/diagrove if you installed the skill.
  • Refunds — refunds are Apple’s decision, at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Your rights under the GDPR

The GDPR gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection over personal data a controller holds about you. We hold none: there is no account to look up, no identifier we could search on, and no database. Your documents are yours, on your devices and in your own iCloud Drive, and you can already read, change, export and delete them there. If you send us an email, and diagnostic reports with it, we keep them only as long as it takes to answer, and you can ask us to delete them at any time.

You can raise a concern with us at support@ninjaapps.net, or lodge a complaint with the Polish supervisory authority, the Urząd Ochrony Danych Osobowych (UODO), at uodo.gov.pl.
