Diagrove/Guide

Keyboard shortcuts and accessibility

Most of Diagrove can be used from the keyboard. This page lists the Mac’s shortcuts menu by menu, then the keys the canvas takes and the ones to hold while you drag, then what changes on iPad with a hardware keyboard. The last part says how Diagrove works with VoiceOver, larger text, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast.

iPad with a hardware keyboard

The iPad’s menu bar holds the same commands, built from the same code, with the same keys: Export… (Shift-Command-E), the Edit, Arrange and Canvas shortcuts above, the zoom keys, View › Show rulers (Command-R), View › Play (Option-Command-P) and Help › Diagrove guide (Command-?). Hold Command to see the shortcuts of the app in front.

What differs:

  • Insert has Picture from Photos… (Shift-Command-I), Picture from Files… and Drawing from Files… (Option-Shift-Command-I).
  • File › Describe a diagram… (Option-Shift-Command-N) starts a diagram from the start screen. There, choose it from the menu bar, because the Files browser keeps the keys; while the template sheet is open, the shortcut works too. In a document it is off.
  • The Mac’s other ways to start (New diagram, New isometric diagram, New from template…), Import…, Page Setup…, Print…, Diagram from sketch or photo…, Command-line tool… and Welcome to Diagrove aren’t in the iPad’s menus. Print… is in the toolbar’s Export menu.

On the canvas, the keys are the Mac’s: the single-key tools, Delete, the arrow keys (with Shift, by 10 points), Return, Shift-H and Shift-V, the digits for opacity, Esc, Tab and Shift-Tab in a table, and the Left Arrow in Play mode. With a trackpad or mouse, Shift and Command do what they do on the Mac while you drag. In the prompt bar, Return sends and Shift-Return starts a new line. In Sketch mode, Esc is Done. See Diagrove on iPad.

VoiceOver

VoiceOver reads Diagrove on the Mac and on iPad.

  • The canvas says what’s on it: “4 graphics, 1 selected”. On the Mac it says the canvas’s name first, such as “Sign-up flow, 4 graphics, 1 selected”. Inside a group you entered it says “Inside a group” first, and lists that group’s graphics.
  • Each graphic says its words, then what it is: “Plan, rectangle”. A line says its label and what it joins, “from Start to Plan”. A table says its size, such as “table, 3 rows, 2 columns”, and the words of its first row; a stencil without words says its stencil’s name. On iPad the graphics come in reading order, top to bottom and left to right, and a thin line can still be found.
  • Changes are announced on the Mac when graphics come, go or are selected; a drag in progress stays quiet.
  • Play mode makes each graphic with an action a button, and says what it does, such as “Go to Home screen”. Pressing it runs the action.
  • A selected table offers Add row and Add column, as its “+” handles do; the handles are buttons too.
  • The tool palette names each tool and says which one is chosen. The Line tool says how new lines are routed, “New lines: Elbow”, and offers the other routings as actions. More shapes says which shape is chosen.
  • The inspector’s sections are headings, each with an action to show or hide it. A number field says its name, value and unit, and VoiceOver can step it up and down. A colour well says its row and its colour: “Fill, light teal”.
  • The sidebar reads a layer as “Layer 1, 13 graphics, hidden” and a canvas as “Checkout, Canvas 1 of 2”. A stencil tile says its name and library; pressing it adds the stencil, an action adds it to your favourites, and VoiceOver’s More content rotor reads its preview.
  • The Outline offers Rename and Add a topic under it on each topic. On iPad, a topic’s chevron says “Show the topics under” or “Hide the topics under” the topic.
  • Sketch mode on iPad: the sketch bar is named Sketch, and Pen and Eraser say which one is chosen. The drawing area says how many strokes it holds, such as “Sketch, 3 strokes”, and takes your finger directly, so you can draw with VoiceOver on.
  • Notices are read out as they arrive. With VoiceOver on, they stay two and a half times as long, up to 25 seconds.

Colours in words

A colour well, and VoiceOver, call a palette colour by its name, such as “light teal”, and a tone by the palette colour nearest it, so you hear “dark slate” rather than a hex code. A gradient is read as “Gradient from light teal to dark slate”. A colour of your own, off the palette, is read as its hex value.

Keyboard navigation

To move through the Welcome window, the template gallery and sheets with Tab, turn on Keyboard navigation in System Settings › Keyboard. Each of the Welcome window’s ways to start then takes Tab and shows a focus ring, and a template grid takes the arrow keys as one control, so a single Tab leaves it. In every sheet, Return presses the main button and Esc cancels.

Text size on iPad

Diagrove’s own text on iPad follows the size you choose in Settings › Display & Brightness › Text Size, and the larger sizes in Settings › Accessibility, up to the largest. Above the default size, inspector labels wrap rather than being cut short, and from the largest standard size they move above their controls. At the accessibility sizes the template gallery opens full screen and notices stay two and a half times as long. The words in your diagrams stay the size you drew them. On the Mac, Diagrove keeps its own sizes.

Reduce Motion

Turn on Reduce Motion in the system’s accessibility settings, on the Mac or the iPad. Diagrove then fades things in place instead of moving them:

  • Notices fade in where they stay, rather than sliding down. That includes the prompt bar’s progress, such as “Reading your words on this Mac…”, and what it drew.
  • The prompt bar’s card for a change on show fades in place, rather than rising from the bar.
  • A zoom that would glide, such as Zoom to fit, lands at once.
  • Entering a group dims the rest of the canvas at once, rather than fading.
  • In Play mode, the outlines a click on nothing shows go at once, rather than fading.
  • Template cards don’t lift under the pointer; their shadow shows instead.

Changes of colour and opacity still fade, because they don’t move anything.

Reduce Transparency

With Reduce Transparency on, the floating tool palette, the play bar and the sketch bar are drawn solid, with an edge, instead of on glass.

Increase Contrast

With Increase Contrast on, the Welcome window and the template gallery give template cards, recent documents and category rows stronger edges, and their captions and counts darker ink. A sheet’s main button gets an edge of its own.
