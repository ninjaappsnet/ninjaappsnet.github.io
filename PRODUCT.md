# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: people deciding whether to download or buy one of the studio's apps. They arrive from the App Store, search, or the studio's social channels (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, X), usually landing on a single app's page rather than the home page. Their job: understand what the app does, see it working, trust the maker, and go to the App Store.

Secondary, served by the same pages: existing users looking for support, a guide topic, a privacy policy or terms (often opened from inside an app), and press looking for the Smalti press kit.

## Product Purpose

ninjaapps.net is the home of NinjaApps, an independent software lab making games and apps for iPhone, iPad and Mac, with web apps planned later. Those web apps will be the studio's own products, not client work. The site exists to sell those products and to carry what the App Store requires (privacy, terms, support URLs). Success: a visitor on an app page understands it in seconds and goes to the App Store, and an existing user finds help without friction.

## Positioning

A one-person-scale lab whose apps are each "honed to do a single thing": native Apple-platform software, no accounts, no tracking, no ads, data on device or in the user's own iCloud. The range (a mosaic restoration game, a work journal, a Mac data-prep and dashboard tool) is part of the claim: one maker, several crafted products.

## Operating Context

- Current products: **Smalti** (game, iPhone; App Store id 6792641328; 411 levels, 10 Rooms, 40 free, a daily mosaic), **Dayfolio** (work journal, iPhone/iPad/Mac, priced tiers), **FlectoBI** (Mac data prep and dashboards).
- Each app has an index page, support, privacy, and (where it sells something) terms. Dayfolio and FlectoBI also have large generated sections: guides, tutorials, a template gallery, articles.
- Some URLs are hardcoded in the shipped app binaries and must never move (see README "Load-bearing URLs").

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static site on GitHub Pages, plain HTML with one shared `style.css` and `assets/theme.js`. No build step for hand-written pages. Guide, template, article and tutorial pages are generated by `tools/dayfolio-build.py` and `tools/dayfolio-templates.py`, and their output is committed.
- `tools/check-site.py` enforces structural contracts: the social footer order and markup, the JSON-LD `sameAs`, legal page versioning, localized privacy pages (12 languages, including Cyrillic, Hangul and Simplified Chinese), stale-claim rules, media rules, and the sitemap. Every redesign must keep it passing.
- No visitor request may leave the origin: fonts are self-hosted, there are no CDNs and no analytics.
- Light and dark themes are both supported, with a manual toggle.
- Published legal pages are versioned and never retro-edited in substance.

## Brand Commitments

- The name **NinjaApps** is fixed.
- Marks (the 忍 seal, the wordmark, the vermillion red) may be evolved or redrawn but must stay recognisable, because the seal is the avatar on all four social channels. Kanji used as decoration is not a commitment.
- Studio frame, app voice: one studio system (type, grid, navigation, footer), with each app page taking its own accent and hero treatment from its app's identity.
- Social footer order YouTube → Instagram → TikTok → X; the TikTok handle `@ninjaapps` is deliberate.
- The marks and copy stay calm in tone ("quiet", "calm"); the copy avoids hype.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real app icons: `assets/icon-*-144.webp` and the per-app media folders.
- Real screenshots and films: `apps/dayfolio/media/` (iPhone, iPad and Mac captures, an iPhone app preview MP4), FlectoBI clips and stills, Smalti screenshots and the press kit.
- Brand sources: `assets/brand/` (seal, wordmark, avatar SVGs, banner and social-card HTML sources).
- No testimonials, reviews, ratings, download counts or press quotes exist; none may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. The products are the hero; the studio is the signature underneath them.
2. Show the app working (real screens, real films) before describing it.
3. Calm and precise over loud: no hype, no fake urgency, no claims the apps don't make.
4. Every page must stay legible as a support and legal document for existing users.
5. Private by design: the site itself tracks nothing and loads nothing from third parties.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast on all new work (the earlier `--ink-45` exception belongs to the retired design and does not carry forward). Localized privacy pages in 12 languages must render in an appropriate script stack. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
